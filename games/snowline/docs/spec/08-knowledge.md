## 8. Knowledge, crafting, and skills

### 8.1 Insights

Recipes and techniques are locked until the survivor has an insight. There are two kinds. A **technique** is how to do something, and is the same in every valley: aim below the fish, bank a fire for the night. A **valley fact** is one of this valley's dealt rules (6.6): the red berries are safe here, the dark stone throws sparks. A returning player already knows the techniques exist and still has to earn every valley fact.

Insights of both kinds come from four sources:

- **Accident:** something unexpected happens while doing something else, such as steel throwing sparks off a particular stone.
- **Observation:** standing still near something for a time. Watching jays feed marks a berry as safe; watching goats shows where a cliff band can be climbed.
- **Failure:** missing fish repeatedly leads to the realisation that the aim must be below them, which sharply raises accuracy.
- **Reflection:** each night's sleep may surface a hint based on what the player has seen but not yet used. This is the in-world hint system for stuck players.

Every insight is recorded in a journal the player can re-read; valley facts also fill in the valley notes chart. Some backgrounds start with an insight or two.

### 8.2 Tool chain

The starting tool is the root of everything.

- Starting tool → cut wood, strip bark, sharpen sticks, butcher game
- Sharpened stick → fish spear (forked tip) → fire-hardened spear
- Plant fibre or a shoelace → cord → snare, bow string, lashings
- Bent sapling + cord → **bow**; straight shoots + fire-hardening + feathers → arrows
- Bark + cord → basket, water vessel
- Hides + cord, dry grass stuffing → **warm layers** for the high country
- Logs + cord → raft (optional: deep-water fishing, crossing the lake)

The raw materials are gathered from things on the ground and from features (6.7). Only what really comes back within weeks does: fallen twigs and branches, driftwood, berries, shellfish, cattails. What doesn't is used up for good after a few takes per tile: a tree's limbs for firewood, its bark (stripped, or shredded for tinder), the lichen on it, a deadfall's breakable wood and the punk in it (the trunk stays), and loose stones. A stripped tree shows it for the rest of the run. **Make** (C, decided 2026-11-26) is a page of the kid's notebook, in its paper and hand: what the kid knows how to make, grouped by what it's for, as the kid would put it (to keep warm tonight; somewhere to stay, out of the wet; water; something to eat; tools and things to carry; so someone finds me), each with a pencil sketch (12.4) and its parts, ticked when carried. It shows only what the kid knows: what any 12–15-year-old would (a fire, a shelter of branches, a sharp stick), what their background taught them (4.2), then each recipe as its technique is learned. **A part is named only as far as the kid knows it** (decided 2026-11-27): a thing they've identified in this valley by its look ("pale lichen"); a thing they know is needed but not which one here, with a question mark ("tinder that catches?", "wood that burns well?"); and where they don't know what's needed at all, only what it's for ("something dry to get it going?", "something to burn?", "a way to start a fire?"). A discovery is never given away: which rock sparks, or that sparks come from rock at all, is found by striking one. An unknown part has no count or tick (the kid can't tell whether what they carry will do) and a question mark in place of its picture. Things for the world (the fire and every build) are chosen here and placed with a preview (12.4, 7.6); the rest are made in the hands. Each craft takes time and can fail at low skill, sometimes consuming materials. Task times depend on the starting tool.

### 8.3 Fire

There are three independent routes to a first fire, so no run depends on one discovery:

1. **Sparks:** striking the steel tool on spark-stone into dry tinder. Which of the valley's stones is the spark-stone, and which tinder catches, are dealt (6.6).
2. **Captured fire:** carrying embers from a lightning-struck snag after a thunderstorm, if the player reaches it in time.
3. **Friction:** a bow drill, which needs cord, the right wood, and a reflection or background insight. Slow and tiring, but it always works eventually. The right wood is dealt (6.6); the wrong one only smokes.

**Laying a fire** (decided 2026-11-26). A fire is laid before it's lit: chosen in Make, placed on open ground (not water, snow, or scree; nothing in the way), and the kid walks beside it, scrapes a spot clear, rings it with stones, and puts in what they carry: tinder, two sticks for kindling, and a piece of wood if they have one (the best they know of). What's missing can be added later. A laid fire is clicked to light it: every way the kid knows of is listed, greyed with what's still needed ("need a piece of the dark speckled stone"); with no way known, just *Light the fire*, greyed: "I need a way to start it". Sparks off steel are listed once the kid knows of them (by striking a rock, or from their background). It can be tried with anything in it, and what goes wrong teaches (decided 2026-11-27): sticks alone won't take a spark or a match (the kid learns it needs something finer first: the tinder-nest technique); tinder with nothing small on it flares and is gone (kindling); a first fire that catches teaches kindling too. A failed try burns the tinder, so more goes in; a fire that catches burns the sticks and wood laid in it. A laid fire can be taken apart again, and isn't walked through.

Once lit, fire has its own rules because so much depends on it.

- It has a fuel level that burns down, faster in wind. Banking it gives long-lasting coals that can be revived in the morning.
- Starting one needs dry tinder; lighting is a skill check that improves quickly with practice.
- Rain puts out a fire that has no cover. Wet wood smokes and burns poorly.
- A burning fire gives warmth and light in a radius, allows cooking, boiling, and fire-hardening, keeps most animals and insects away, and raises morale.
- Embers can be carried in a bark container to light a fire elsewhere.
- Fire does not spread to the forest in v1.

### 8.4 Skills

Five skills, each level 0–5: firecraft, foraging, fishing, hunting, crafting. They rise with use, including failed attempts. Higher skill means better success odds and shorter task times. There are no skill points to spend.
