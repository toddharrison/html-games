## 13.2 Still open

Raised while building, each with the simple option taken so work could go on (2026-11-25). Each is for discussion; changing the choice later is cheap unless noted.

- **Q19. What a save holds.** 11.6 says a save is the differences from the freshly generated world. *Taken:* the whole game state without the deal and the map (about 40 KB, mostly the explored bitset). Simpler and safer; worth revisiting only if saves get large.
- **Q20. What a taste tells.** *Taken:* a taste of a nourishing or a poor plant feels the same ("did no harm", suspected safe); only eating a handful tells them apart. A sickening or deadly plant is known from a taste.
- **Q21. Watching before there are jays.** *Taken:* until animals exist (M4), "watch the patch" is an action: 40 minutes in daylight, after which jays have eaten the plant or left it alone, giving a suspicion. When jays arrive, should watching stay an action, or only happen by standing still near them?
- **Q22. Each background's valley fact.** The spec names three. *Taken:* Scout knows the bow-drill wood, Angler the foul water, Hunter's kid one animal's temper, Farm kid one plant, Runner one stone, Reader one remedy. The Angler's is strong (it settles a whole row); is that right?
- **Q23. How fast sleep runs.** *Taken:* 90× (a night's sleep in about 4 real seconds), by running ticks.
- **Q24. The HUD's vitals.** *Taken:* six small marks in the top right (♥ ◊ ● ☾ ≈ ☼) with four pips each, which change colour and pulse when low, plus words for what the survivor feels. Is that the right amount of information on screen?
- **Q25. Where each dealt wood and tinder comes from.** *Taken:* resinous wood from conifers, white-barked from aspens, grey twisted from stunted trees, red-barked from deadfall, pale soft from driftwood on the shore; dry grass from meadows, inner bark from aspens, hanging lichen from trees above the valley floor, seed fluff from reeds, punk wood from deadfall. The names are the spec's appearances; only their sources were chosen.
- **Q26. What burning teaches about wood.** *Taken:* long-burning, smoky, and quick woods show themselves when burned; the drill wood burns "plain" (a suspicion), and only the bow drill confirms it; a wrong wood in the drill becomes "not for the drill".
- **Q27. Sharpening a stick.** *Taken:* everyone knows it from the start, so the tool chain can begin on day 1; Scouts also know the bow drill (the spec's "background insight").
- **Q28. Where camp improvements go.** *Reopened 2026-11-26:* builds become objects drawn in the world, placed with a preview and going up in stages (M7). *Taken until then:* walls, a bed, and a lean-to belong to the camp tile (the one slept on) rather than being drawn as separate walls round it. Reflector stones go on a fire, a cache on a tree, the signal pile on open ground.
- **Q29. A fire on the tile.** *Taken:* a fire takes a tile and can't be walked through; it is laid on the faced tile.
- **Q30. How signs show and are judged.** *Taken:* every three hours a foretelling sign shows 70% of the time if rain or a storm comes within 9 hours (4% otherwise); a noise sign 10%. Each is judged 12 hours later; two judgements make a suspicion, four consistent ones make it known. Is learning the signs too slow or too quick?
- **Q31. Meeting a dangerous animal.** *Taken:* it warns for about a minute; facing means standing still facing it the whole time; noise is a *Shout* action; fire means a burning fire within 3 tiles. Backing off well beyond its distance always works. An attack is a wound and a blow, not death outright. Watching from 6–12 tiles for 20 minutes teaches its boldness and warning, but only meeting it teaches the deterrent.
- **Q32. The cougar.** *Taken:* it only follows at dusk or night when the survivor is weak (health, food, or rest low), alone, and more than 10 tiles from a fire, and then closes to a warning. It is otherwise never seen.
- **Q33. When winter ends a run.** *Taken:* day 72, twelve days after the snow reaches the lake, if the cold hasn't already.
- **Q34. Fish.** *Decided 2026-11-26:* trout are fish drawn in the water (7.4). The density per tile stays as how many fish a tile holds, for the line and the weir.
- **Q35. Sound.** *Taken:* synthesized in the browser (no recorded audio), with ambient loops now and effects and music in M6. Good enough to keep, or record real sounds before release?
- **Q36. The "watch the patch" action once jays exist.** *Taken:* both: jays now visibly eat safe plants (a suspicion when seen), and the watch action stays as the deliberate, slower way.
- **Q37. How likely a signal is seen.** *Taken:* the best smoky fire scores 0.25, plus 0.12 a band up, 0.3 on the knoll, 0.1 for a lit signal pile, 0.08 in the open; a fire without smoke 0.03; the mirror adds 0.25 in clear weather; late flights see 70% as well. A smoky signal fire on the knoll is all but certain; a smoky campfire by the lake about one in four.
- **Q38. What the severe storm breaks.** *Taken:* lean-tos, walls, beds, woodpiles, signal piles, and snares; reflector stones, the hanging cache, and the weir stand. About a hundred trees across the valley fall and become deadfall (passable, burnable). Fires go out, though a banked one may survive.
- **Q39. The in-development notice.** 11.1 says it stays until a run can end in rescue. *Taken:* removed from the title and the landing page at M5, though M6's polish (effects, music, the art pass) isn't done. Put it back until M6?
- **Q40. Balance targets.** The bot's results are in docs/TODO.md. What should a careful first-time player's run look like: rescued by the beacon most of the time, or more often not? That decides whether the cold and the storm want easing further.
- **Q41. Tuning made during the balance pass.** *Taken:* drying, chill, and hypothermia were eased (FEATURES §23) because nights were killing a sheltered, fed survivor on day 2. Rest and hunger were left alone.
- **Q42. Turning in place.** *Taken:* Shift and a direction turns without stepping. Lighting a fire needs the survivor to face open ground, and nothing else turned them.
- **Q43. Who draws the final art.** The M6 pass draws every feature, animal, and the survivor as sprites in code; terrain is still a flat colour with a glyph. Keep this, have Claude draw terrain tiles too, or bring in an artist (the renderer can take an atlas image instead, SPEC 12.1)? *2026-11-26:* Claude draws terrain too, in the beauty pass (12.1); an artist is still a later option.
- **Q44. The music.** *Taken:* four short synthesized pieces (first fire, first wide view, first snow, rescue), played once each per run. Right moments? Real instruments before release?
- **Q45. The version at release.** *Taken (2026-11-26):* the first release is 0.0.1; from there, semver as the repo's rules say.
- **Q46. Free walking or grid steps.** *Being tried:* the survivor walks freely over the tile grid (5.2), behind one switch (`FREE_WALK`). Keep it, and rewrite 5.2 and the step tests for it, or go back to grid steps?
